## Methodological note (locked for transparency)

The pre-reg's operational test (UCDP type-2 strife ≥50/year) is **narrower than the mechanism it was designed to test**. UCDP's type_of_violence coding:
- Type 1 (state-based): government vs organized armed group → Govt-Togo vs JNIM
- Type 2 (non-state): non-state armed group vs non-state armed group → e.g., JNIM vs IS-Sahel, or communal violence
- Type 3 (one-sided): armed group targets civilians → JNIM vs Togolese civilians

The MLI/BFA/NER in-sample anchors all crossed type-2 because they had:
- Self-defense militias forming and fighting JNIM (Dozos in MLI, Koglweogo in BFA) → NSA-vs-NSA → type 2
- IS-Sahel competing with JNIM → NSA-vs-NSA → type 2
- Inter-ethnic communal violence (Dogon-Fulani, Tuareg factional) → communal type 2

Togo currently has JNIM violence WITHOUT these secondary actors (no self-defense militias yet; no IS-Sahel presence; no documented communal). So UCDP type-2 threshold may not formally cross even though the broader armed-conflict mechanism is active.

**The RULAC NIAC classification captures what the UCDP type-2 threshold was designed to proxy** — namely "Togo has joined the multi-actor armed conflict cohort that MLI/BFA/NER have."

This is a meaningful methodological learning, not a pre-reg failure. The pre-reg's choice of UCDP type-2 was reasonable based on in-sample anchors. The cross-validation via RULAC strengthens the broader claim while exposing the operational threshold's blind spot.
