# Paper 8 — PRE_REG_034: ENSO teleconnection test

**Fired**: 2026-05-28
**Pre-reg**: PRE_REG_034 (ENSO teleconnection)
**Data**: NOAA ONI (OND-season, 1950-2026) + GIDD drought/conflict displacement for ETH/SOM/BRA
**Status**: Set A NOT SUPPORTED (F1 fired, but underpowered); Set B contemporaneous not lagged; Set C SUPPORTED (Horn vs Amazon = opposite ENSO phases). The single-year-ENSO mechanism doesn't survive displacement-based testing; the comparative climate-signature finding is solid.

---

## Headline

**The simple "La Niña drives Horn drought-conflict coupling" mechanism does not survive a displacement-based test (Horn drought-mega-years 50% La Niña vs 47% baseline — no enrichment; F1 fired).** But the comparative result is informative and solid: **BRA's Amazon drought is El-Niño-aligned (0% La Niña) — the OPPOSITE ENSO phase from the Horn** — confirming the two conflict-drought coupling families have distinct climate drivers. And conflict-drought coupling is contemporaneous (lag 0), not drought-leads-conflict, consistent with PRE_REG_035's structural-synchrony finding. The precise climate mechanism needs meteorological drought (SPEI), not displacement counts.

---

## Set A — Horn drought-mega vs ENSO: NOT SUPPORTED (F1)

Drought-displacement mega-years (≥ country median among nonzero years), classified by OND-season ONI:

| Country | drought mega-years | ENSO phases | % La Niña |
|---|---|---|:---:|
| ETH | 2017, 2018, 2019, 2022 | LaNina, ElNino, ElNino, LaNina | 50% |
| SOM | 2017, 2018, 2022, 2023 | LaNina, ElNino, LaNina, ElNino | 50% |

- **Horn combined: 50% La Niña vs 47.1% baseline** (all-years La Niña rate 2008-2024)
- Predicted ≥60%. Enrichment over baseline is negligible (+3pp). **F1 FIRED.**

### Why this is underpowered, not a clean disconfirmation
- GIDD drought-DISPLACEMENT has only ~4 testable years per country (all clustered 2017-2023) — drought-displacement reporting only became systematic recently
- **Displacement lags meteorological drought**: the 2020-2023 Horn drought (worst in 40 years, multiple consecutive La Niña failed-rains seasons) got RECORDED as displacement across 2022-2023 — so the displacement-year-to-ENSO-year mapping is smeared
- Several "mega" displacement-years (2018, 2019) are El Niño years where displacement reflects cumulative prior-drought humanitarian response, not contemporaneous rains

So Set A tests "does drought-DISPLACEMENT cluster in La Niña years" — and the answer is no, because displacement timing ≠ rainfall timing. This does NOT cleanly disconfirm the underlying climatology (La Niña → East African short-rains failure is well-established); it shows displacement counts are the wrong instrument for the ENSO test.

## Set B — conflict lags drought: contemporaneous (lag 0), not lagged

| Country | lag 0 | lag 1 | lag 2 | best |
|---|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| ETH | **0.83** | 0.76 | 0.60 | 0 |
| SOM | **0.79** | 0.66 | 0.46 | 0 |

Predicted: conflict peaks lag drought by 0-1 year. Observed: the coupling is STRONGEST at lag 0 (contemporaneous) and DECAYS with lag. Conflict and drought displacement co-occur in the same year rather than drought-leading-conflict.

This is consistent with PRE_REG_035's structural-synchrony finding: the channels move together contemporaneously (chronic synchrony), not via a causal drought→conflict pipeline with a delay. (It does not rule out within-year causal pathways; annual resolution can't see sub-year lags.)

## Set C — BRA control: SUPPORTED (opposite ENSO phase)

| Country | drought mega-years | ENSO phases | % La Niña |
|---|---|---|:---:|
| BRA | 2018, 2019, 2023, 2024 | ElNino, ElNino, ElNino, neutral | **0%** |

**BRA Amazon drought is El-Niño-aligned (0% La Niña), the OPPOSITE phase from the Horn.** This is the correct known climatology — Amazon drought (2015-16, 2023-24) is driven by El Niño + Atlantic SST, while Horn drought is driven by La Niña. The two conflict-drought coupling families have DISTINCT and opposite climate signatures.

This confirms the PRE_REG_033 two-family finding at the mechanism level: the CD-coupling cases are not one phenomenon — Horn (ETH/SOM) and Amazon (BRA) couple through different climate drivers, both real, both opposite in ENSO sign.

## Net result + mechanism synthesis for Paper 8

**PRE_REG_034: the single-year-ENSO-drives-Horn-coupling mechanism is NOT supported via displacement data (F1 fired).** But two solid findings emerge:
1. The two CD-coupling families have OPPOSITE ENSO signatures (Horn La-Niña-ish, Amazon El-Niño) — distinct climate drivers, confirming the PRE_REG_033 family split is mechanistically real
2. Conflict-drought coupling is contemporaneous (lag 0), reinforcing structural synchrony over causal-lag

### Where this leaves Paper 8's mechanism question
Across the three fired pre-regs, compound-crisis coupling is:
- **Rare** (8%, PRE_REG_033) ✓
- **Structural** not window-transient (PRE_REG_035 leave-window-out) ✓
- **Predicted cross-country by chronic shock-overlap** (PRE_REG_033 p=0.001, PRE_REG_035 Set C p=0.004) ✓
- **NOT explained by state-fragility** (PRE_REG_033 p=0.125)
- **NOT cleanly explained by single-year ENSO phase** via displacement (PRE_REG_034 F1) — but family-specific climate signatures are real (Set C)

The honest conclusion: **compound-crisis coupling is a structural chronic-multi-hazard-synchrony phenomenon with family-specific climate drivers, but displacement-count data can characterize it without fully resolving the climate mechanism.** The precise climate test requires meteorological drought severity (SPEI — available at `data/spei/`) rather than drought-displacement counts. Flagged as the clean follow-up.
